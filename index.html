Summary & Conclusion

Course Preparation With an AI Knowledge Base

We compared how ADS401 postgraduate students used XJTLU AI to prepare for class. The study examined access to verifiable sources, usability and workload.

Research Questions

Main question. How does using XJTLU AI with an ADS401 course knowledge base (KB+) compare with using general XJTLU AI chat (KB−) when postgraduate students prepare for class?

  1. Task performance. Do the two groups differ in the quality of their course content reviews and course designs?
  2. User experience. How do the two groups differ in perceived usability, the ability to find verifiable sources, and willingness to continue using the tool?
  3. Workload and efficiency. How do the two groups differ in perceived workload, time spent on each task, and the number of follow-up questions?
  4. Task suitability. Which tasks benefit most from the course knowledge base, and when do answers lack relevance, accuracy, or sufficient detail?

The Round 2 analysis uses 24 post-task questionnaires to describe students’ experiences with the redesigned interface. Task timings and chat logs would help assess actual efficiency.

Study Design

Both groups used XJTLU AI. The study design specified the same underlying model, task briefs, materials and initial prompts.

KB+ · Course knowledge base

Participants used XJTLU AI with the ADS401 course knowledge base attached.

KB− · General AI chat

Participants used general XJTLU AI chat without a course knowledge base.

Task 1 · Understanding and Evaluating Course Content

Participants received a set of ADS401 course excerpts covering concepts, case examples and questions. They used AI to complete four steps.

  1. Summarise the key points and outline the overall structure of the materials.
  2. Explain two or three unfamiliar concepts or terms from the materials.
  3. Evaluate how well one argument or proposed solution in the materials is justified.
  4. Write three questions to guide preparation or check understanding.

Task 2 · Systematic Course Design

Participants received an ADS401-related course or system design brief specifying the target audience, course duration, and topic scope.

  1. Course objectives and intended learning outcomes.
  2. Content modules and how the knowledge was organised.
  3. Teaching activities and assessment methods.
  4. Supporting materials or rationale for each module.

Pre-task questionnaire. The study design included questions about students’ backgrounds, preparation habits and expectations for AI-assisted preparation.

Post-task questionnaire. Participants rated usability, user experience and workload. The analysis used 24 completed questionnaires and grouped respondents by the chat condition they reported using.

Results

Round 2, after the interface redesign. Among 24 respondents, students who used the course knowledge base (KB+) rated the system as more usable and reported greater intention to continue using it than students who used general chat (KB−).

Group means, with 12 respondents per group. Each bar starts at the minimum of its scale.

Key Findings

  • Usability. Mean SUS scores were 89.17 for KB+ and 66.46 for KB−. The medians were 87.50 and 66.25, respectively.
  • User experience. KB+ respondents rated ease of use and course relevance higher. They also reported stronger intention to continue and less concern about over-reliance.
  • Other ratings. The analysis did not detect statistically significant differences in perceived usefulness, overall satisfaction or confidence in understanding the material.

Limitations and Next Steps

Study Limitations

With 24 respondents (12 per group), the study had limited power to detect small-to-moderate differences. The results should be interpreted as preliminary evidence from this sample.

The questionnaire captured self-reported experiences. Task efficiency and learning gains were outside its scope.

Next Steps

Verify the NASA-TLX scores against the original item responses. A larger follow-up study could record task timings and assess the quality of participants’ work.

Summary and Conclusion

In the second round, respondents using the course knowledge base (KB+) reported higher usability than those using general chat (KB−) (mean SUS 89.17 vs 66.46, Holm-adjusted p < .0001). They also rated ease of use and course relevance higher, reported greater intention to continue using the tool, and expressed less concern about over-reliance.

The analysis did not detect statistically significant differences in perceived usefulness, overall satisfaction or confidence in understanding the material.

Product Recommendations

Integrate the agent’s course knowledge base retrieval and question answering directly into the main XJTLU AI chat.

  1. One-click control. Let students attach or detach the course knowledge base in the chat.
  2. Visible fallback. Tell students when retrieval finds no relevant course material and clearly label any answer from general chat.
  3. Page-level citations. Cite the specific source page for each claim drawn from course materials.
